19.

Quarantine. One of the ways to treat an epidemic is to keep the infected away from the susceptible; this is called quarantine. The intention is to reduce the chance that the illness will be transmitted to a susceptible person. Thus, quarantine alters the transmission coefficient.
🔗

(a)

Suppose a quarantine is put into effect that cuts in half the chance that a susceptible will fall ill. What is the new transmission coefficient?
🔗
🔗

(b)

In Subsubsection 1.1.6.9 it was determined that whenever there were fewer than 7143 susceptibles, the number of infected would decline instead of grow. We called 7143 a threshold level for \(S\text{.}\) Changing the transmission coefficient, as in part (a), changes the threshold level for \(S\text{.}\) What is the new threshold?
🔗
🔗

(c)

Suppose we start with \(S\) = 45,400. Does quarantine eliminate the epidemic, in the sense that the number of infected immediately goes down from 2100, without ever showing an increase in the number of cases?
🔗
🔗

(d)

Since the new transmission coefficient is not small enough to guarantee that \(I\) never goes up, can you find a smaller value that does guarantee \(I\) never goes up? Continue to assume we start with \(S = 45400\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(e)

Suppose the initial susceptible population is 45,400. What is the largest value that the transmission coefficient can have and still guarantee that \(I\) never goes up? What level of quarantine does this represent? That is, do you have to reduce the chance that a susceptible will fall ill to one-third of what it was with no quarantine at all, to one-fourth, or what?
