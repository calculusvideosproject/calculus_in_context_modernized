11.

Back to the circle. Consider the unit circle centered at the origin. Pythagoras’ Theorem shows that a point \((x, y)\) is on the circle if and only if \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\text{.}\) If we solve this for \(y\) in terms of \(x\text{,}\) we get \(y=\pm \sqrt{1 - x^2}\text{,}\) where the plus sign gives us the upper half of the circle and the minus sign gives the lower half. This suggests that we look at the function \(g(x) = \sqrt{1 - x^2}\text{.}\) The arclength of \(g(x)\) over the interval \(-1 \leq x \leq 1\) should then be exactly \(\pi\text{.}\)
