Exercise 9.

Graph the function \(f(x) = 1 - 2 x^2\) on the interval \(-1 \leq x \leq 1\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

What is the \(y\)-intercept of this graph? The graph has two \(x\)-intercepts; use algebra to find them.
🔗
You can also find an \(x\)-intercept using the computer. The idea is to
🔗
magnify the graph near the intercept until you can determine as many decimal places in the \(x\) coordinate as you want. For a start, graph the function on the interval \(0\leq x\leq1\text{.}\) You should be able to see that the graph on your computer monitor crosses the \(x\)-axis somewhere around .7. Regraph \(f(x)\) on the interval \(.6 \leq x \leq .8\text{.}\) You should then be able to determine that the \(x\)-intercept lies between .70 and .71. This means \(x =\) .7…; that is, you know the location of the \(x\)-intercept to one decimal place of accuracy.
🔗
🔗

(b)

Regraph \(f(x)\) on the interval \(.70 \leq x \leq .71\) to get two decimal places of accuracy in the location of the \(x\)-intercept. Continue this process until you have at least 7 places of accuracy. What is the \(x\)-intercept?
